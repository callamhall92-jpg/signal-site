How to find UK public-sector construction tenders

Every public-sector tender in the UK is published free of charge on an official website. Which website depends on where the buyer is and how big the contract is. This guide sets out where to look.

Checked against GOV.UK and legislation.gov.uk on 16 November 2026.

England, Wales and Northern Ireland: Find a Tender

Since the Procurement Act 2023 came into force on 24 February 2025, Find a Tender is the central place where public bodies in England, Wales and Northern Ireland publish their notices. The government calls it the central digital platform. It carries:

  • Contracts above the main thresholds. For most public works contracts the threshold is £5,193,000 including VAT for procurements started on or after 1 January 2026.
  • Smaller advertised contracts. If a public body advertises a contract worth at least £30,000 including VAT (£12,000 for central government bodies and NHS trusts), it must publish a below-threshold tender notice on Find a Tender before advertising it anywhere else.
  • Planned procurements and awards. Notices about upcoming work and notices saying who won a contract are published there too.

Before February 2025, smaller contracts in England were published on Contracts Finder. It still holds older notices and some buyers still post there, so it remains worth searching.

Scotland: Public Contracts Scotland

Scotland has its own rules, set by the Procurement Reform (Scotland) Act 2014 and related regulations. Scottish public bodies publish on Public Contracts Scotland. The 2014 Act's rules apply to works contracts from £2,000,000 and to goods and services contracts from £50,000, though many buyers advertise smaller contracts there too.

Wales: Sell2Wales

Welsh public bodies follow the Procurement Act 2023, with some Welsh-specific regulations, and publish on Find a Tender. Many also publish on Sell2Wales, which is worth checking for smaller Welsh contracts.

Northern Ireland: eTendersNI

Northern Ireland public bodies publish on Find a Tender and also use eTendersNI for their tender documents.

Work that never appears as a public tender

  • Frameworks. Much repair, maintenance and capital work goes to firms already on a framework, through call-offs that are not advertised again. If the same few firms keep winning a buyer's work, ask when the framework next opens. See frameworks and call-offs explained.
  • Very small contracts. Below £30,000 (£12,000 for central government), a buyer does not have to publish a notice at all. Many councils keep approved contractor lists or use quick quotes for this work.
  • Subcontracting. Main contractors who win large public contracts often look for local subcontractors. Award notices show who won and are a good list of firms to approach.
