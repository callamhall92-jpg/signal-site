Writing a tender response that scores well

Public-sector tenders are marked against published criteria, often by several evaluators working separately. The firms that win are rarely the best writers. They are the ones that answer exactly what was asked, prove every claim, and make the evaluator's job easy.

Checked against GOV.UK guidance on 17 November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.

Start with how it is marked

Under the Procurement Act 2023, buyers award contracts to the most advantageous tender, judged against award criteria they must publish in advance with their relative importance. Before you write anything, find three things in the tender documents:

  • The weighting between quality, price and social value. A 70% quality and 30% price split rewards a strong written response far more than shaving your rates.
  • The scoring scale for each question, typically 0 to 5 or 0 to 10, with a description of what earns each mark. The top band usually asks for something like "full confidence, with evidence and added value".
  • Word or page limits, and any minimum score you must reach on a question to stay in the running.

Answer every part of every question

Many questions hide three or four separate requirements in one paragraph. Break each question into its parts, use them as your headings, and answer them in the same order. Evaluators score against a checklist, and an answer that skips one part rarely reaches the top band.

Write for this contract, not for your company in general. "We have 15 years' experience" scores less than "On a similar £180,000 school roof replacement for a neighbouring council in 2025, we phased the work around term dates and finished two weeks early."

Prove it

  • Named projects with client, value, date and outcome.
  • Numbers: response times you hit, defects at handover, complaints, repeat work from the same client.
  • Named people with their role and qualifications, if the buyer asks who will run the work.
  • Your actual method: how you will programme, manage residents or pupils, control quality and keep the site safe on this job.

Take social value seriously

Central government bodies must give social value at least 10% of the total score under PPN 002, and its use became mandatory for procurements under the Procurement Act from 1 October 2025. Other public bodies, including the NHS and many councils, apply a similar weighting. Every social value commitment a winning supplier makes is written into the contract as a term or a measured target, so promise only what you will deliver.

For a small firm, strong and realistic social value commitments often include:

  • An apprentice or trainee on the contract, or work placements for local school leavers.
  • Buying materials and subcontracting from local suppliers within a stated distance.
  • Paying your own subcontractors within 30 days.
  • Practical help for the community you are working in, such as a day's work on a local community building.
  • Reducing vehicle miles and waste on the job, with how you will measure it.

Price it properly

Complete the pricing schedule exactly as set out, with no missing rates or added conditions. Qualifications and assumptions can lead to a bid being rejected. An abnormally low price can be investigated and rejected too, so price the work at a level you can deliver safely and profitably. If anything is unclear, ask a clarification question before the deadline: the buyer will usually share the answer with all bidders.

Check before you submit

  • Every question answered, within the word limits.
  • Every mandatory document attached and in date.
  • Names, dates and the buyer's name correct throughout, especially if you reused text from an old bid.
  • Submitted through the portal at least a day early. Portals slow down near deadlines, and late bids are almost never accepted.

Use the feedback

When the contract is awarded, the Procurement Act requires buyers to send an assessment summary: unsuccessful bidders receive their own scores and the winning bidder's. Compare your answers with the marks, note where you dropped points, and fix it in your standard text before the next bid.
