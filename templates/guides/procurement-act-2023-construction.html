The Procurement Act 2023: what changed for construction firms

The Procurement Act 2023 replaced the old Public Contracts Regulations for public bodies in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Here is what changed for firms looking for public construction and maintenance work.

Checked against GOV.UK guidance and legislation.gov.uk on 16 November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice. Scotland has its own rules and is not covered by the Act's main procurement procedures.

When it applies

The Act came into force on 24 February 2025. Procurements started before then continue under the old rules, so you will see both kinds of notice for some time.

One place to look

Notices under the Act are published on the central digital platform, which is the Find a Tender service. This includes smaller contracts: a public body that advertises a contract worth at least £30,000 including VAT (£12,000 for central government bodies and NHS trusts) must publish a below-threshold tender notice there before advertising it anywhere else. Previously those smaller contracts went on Contracts Finder.

Thresholds include VAT

All thresholds under the Act include VAT. For procurements started on or after 1 January 2026, the main threshold for most works contracts is £5,193,000. Above it, the full procurement rules apply; below it, a lighter set of rules applies.

Supplier registration

Suppliers bidding under the Act register their core details once on the central digital platform and share them with buyers, rather than filling in the same information for every bid.

Frameworks can reopen

The Act introduced open frameworks, which allow new suppliers to join at set points over up to eight years. Ordinary frameworks are generally limited to four years. See frameworks and call-offs explained.

Small and local suppliers

For below-threshold contracts, the Act allows buyers to reserve a contract for suppliers based in the UK, or in a particular county or area of the UK. This was not possible under the old regulations. Watch the notice for any such reservation.

More notices about planned and finished work

Buyers now publish more notices across a contract's life, including about planned procurements, awards and contract performance. That means more advance warning of work coming up, and more information about who won.
